Work / Unit 7

Unit 7 / Creating Digital Video

A game review of Phasmophobia, planned end to end

A five to ten minute review, made as an employee of Media Dog Solutions. Nine planning documents, a month of captured gameplay logged scene by scene, and a written evaluation afterwards.

Made and evaluated

The brief

I was set the role of an employee of Media Dog Solutions, a fictional company written into the assignment, producing a game review for their channel. The video had to be between five and ten minutes, use original footage, and include analysis, gameplay and commentary.

I picked Phasmophobia because, in my own words at the time:

Phasmophobia was chosen for its unique blend of horror and teamwork. The design allows for engaging footage, varied reactions, and analysis of mechanics. It meets the brief by combining original content with commentary and structured review.

My treatment, design justification

This is the most thoroughly planned thing I have made. The section below is the paperwork, in the order I produced it.

Treatment: deciding what the video was for

The treatment fixed the purpose, audience, tone and runtime before anything else happened.

  • Purpose: To explore how Phasmophobia creates fear through sound design, teamwork, and unpredictable ghost behavior. The review will help viewers decide if the game suits their interests.
  • Audience: Teenagers and young adults (ages 15–30) who enjoy horror games, cooperative gameplay, and immersive experiences. The video will avoid graphic content and use clear warnings to ensure age-appropriate presentation.
  • Genre: horror, co-op multiplayer, psychological thriller, simulation.
  • Runtime: between five and ten minutes.

It also set out what I would need (a PC with the game, screen recording software, a microphone, editing software and royalty-free sound), four contingencies, and a four-week schedule: research and script writing, then recording gameplay and voiceover, then editing and effects, then final review and submission.

One of the four contingencies I never finished. Against Weak hardware I wrote (solution: ???) and moved on. Hardware speed then turns up in my evaluation as one of the things that actually limited the project, so it was the right risk to spot and the wrong one to leave blank.

Script: ten sections

  1. The Calm Before the Chaos
  2. The Maps – Every Haunt Feels Different
  3. Investigation and Chaos
  4. Tools of the Trade
  5. Sanity – The Mind vs. the Supernatural
  6. The Cursed Objects
  7. The Hunt Begins
  8. Visuals, Updates, and Replayability
  9. Fear Meets Fun
  10. Final Verdict

It opens:

Hi everyone! Today we're diving into the paranormal madness of Phasmophobia, the game where you and your friends pretend to be professional ghost hunters… and then immediately prove you're not.

So grab your flashlight, stock up on sanity pills, and remember: in Phasmophobia, the ghosts aren't your biggest problem — your friends are.

Closing lines

Storyboard: eight panels

Eight panels, each with a shot description rather than a formal camera angle.

  1. Ready for Chaos. Every hunt starts with confidence... and absolutely zero idea what we're doing.
  2. Entering the House. A creepy, dark suburban home; flashlights barely work; one player's already regretting their life choices.
  3. Looking for Clues. The team checking fingerprints, spirit box, freezing temps, D.O.T.S — one person screaming at a closet.
  4. First Ghost Encounter. Lights flicker, a ghostly figure manifests behind a player while another hides behind a chair.
  5. Running for Our Lives. Everyone sprinting toward the van as the ghost hunts; one drops the camera dramatically.
  6. Aftermath in the Van. The surviving players stare at the monitor, realizing one friend didn't make it.
  7. Sanity Slipping. Sanity drops, lights flicker, and fear replaces confidence. The tension quietly builds.
  8. Tempting Fate. A cursed object is used. Sanity drains, tension spikes, and the ghost turns aggressive.

The storyboard file has two slides carrying the same eight panels, but the last two are in opposite order between them, and one slide has four more images than the other. Nothing in the file says which is the final version, so I have used the order above and flagged it rather than guessing.

The panel order also does not match my script or my shooting log, both of which put Sanity and Cursed Objects before The Hunt Begins. Getting those three documents to agree with each other is something I would do next time before filming rather than after.

Three things wrong with my own risk assessment

I only noticed these writing this page up, and they are worth leaving visible.

  • Two ratings do not multiply out. Hazards 5 and 6 are both scored severity 2 and likelihood 2, which is 4. The form prints 6 and 5. The 5 is not even a possible product of two numbers on the form's own scale.
  • Hazard 1 has no description in the main table. The row is scored, but the hazard column is empty. It is only the controls table underneath that names it as tripping over cables.
  • The form is unsigned. It says at the top that a tutor must sign it before filming starts. My signature line, the date and the tutor signature are all blank.

The hazards themselves I still think were the right ones. Two of the controls, backing up regularly and building a realistic schedule, are habits I kept.

Cast and crew

Six of the seven crew roles were mine: director, camera person, crewperson, editor, scriptwriter and voiceover technician. The seventh, sound designer, was not a person at all. My own crew list records it as Use royalty-free libraries, which is honest about how the audio was actually put together.

The narrator is an AI text-to-speech voice, not me. There was also an optional guest slot for a family member, which I have left unnamed here.

The log also carries a visuals, audio, dialogue and editing-notes column for each scene, which is where the actual direction lives: Fade in with dark house exterior, slow camera pan for the intro, Add camera shake for intensity on the sanity scene, Add heartbeat overlay on the hunt.

What is wrong with it

  • Scene 1 is dated 28/20/25. There is no month 20. I have left it as written rather than quietly correcting it to October.
  • Scene 9 is the only row with no status, even though it has a date, four filenames and a timecode that sits in the middle of the chain. It was clearly shot and placed.
  • Scene 10, Secrets & Easter Eggs, is marked completed but has no date, no filenames and no timecode, and is not part of the chain. It also has no matching section anywhere in my script.
  • Scene 6's title stops after an ampersand.

None of these would survive a second pass. Keeping the log at all was the right call: it is the only reason I can tell you exactly what is in the cut and how long each part runs.

Evaluation, in my own words

The purpose of my video was to review Phasmophobia by informing viewers about the gameplay, objectives, and overall experience, while also entertaining them. The video aimed to help viewers decide whether the game is something they would enjoy playing.

On purpose

The feedback included comments that the project was very good, well structured, and interesting. Some viewers suggested clearer speech, slightly shorter length, and better clarity in some explanations. Based on this feedback, I focused on improving audio clarity and pacing during editing to make the video easier to follow.

On feedback and what I changed

Yes, limitations included background noise, time restrictions, slow hardware and a limited amount of recorded footage. These factors meant I had fewer clips to choose from, but I managed this by selecting the strongest gameplay moments and editing carefully.

On limitations

If I completed this project again, I would plan recordings more carefully, improve my setup, to avoid unexpected bugs and errors in my video editing program and gather feedback earlier so I could make improvements sooner.

On what I would do differently

I also justified the changes I made against the original design: I justified changes such as cutting unnecessary sections, improving audio, and refining commentary because they improved clarity and engagement.

Two things I need to supply

  • The finished video is not in this portfolio. The only video file in my coursework folder is the walkthrough of my Unit 13 website. The review itself needs finding and adding, so this page can show it rather than describe it.
  • Which tools I actually used. My treatment names OBS Studio for capture and DaVinci Resolve for editing as the tools I planned to use, and my evaluation only ever says my video editing program. CapCut text-to-speech is confirmed by my asset list and crew list. I should record the rest properly rather than leave it to be inferred from a plan.
