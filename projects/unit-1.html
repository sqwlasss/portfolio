Unit 1 / The Online World

How the internet and online services actually work

A mandatory, externally assessed unit. There is no coursework for it, so this page sets out what the unit covers rather than pretending to show work that does not exist.

Unit studied

Why this page has no coursework on it

Unit 1 is assessed by external examination, not by assignment. There is no brief, no tasks and no submitted work, because that is not how the unit is marked. An earlier version of this page invented three tasks, a grade and three tutor comments for it. All of that has gone.

What follows is the content I studied, taken from the unit specification. It is the most useful honest thing this page can be, and a lot of it is directly relevant to the web work elsewhere in this portfolio.

Learning aims

  1. Investigate online services and online communication.
  2. Investigate components of the internet and how digital devices exchange and store information.
  3. Investigate issues with operating online.

What the unit covers

A: Online services and communication

  • Online services

    What they are and why they are used

    • Communication: email, instant messaging, newsgroups, social networking, online conferencing, blogs, chat rooms
    • Real-time information: timetables, news, traffic, flight status, weather
    • Commerce: internet banking, auctions, retail, publishing
    • Government: tax returns, e-voting, applications for services and grants
    • Education and virtual learning environments
    • Business: video conferencing, collaborative working, business networks
    • Entertainment and download services
    • Online advertising, the affiliate pay-per-click model, and online data storage

  • Online documents

    Working on files together

    • Why files are compressed for upload and download, and how
    • Online versus standalone software for creating documents
    • Collaborative working, sharing and automatic backup
    • Version control, levels of access and file permissions: read only, read/write, full control

  • Online communication

    How and why it is used

    • Blogs, wikis and podcasting
    • Virtual learning environments in education
    • Social networking and online communities
    • Instant messaging and the client and server roles behind it
    • Real-time speech and video over the internet, including VoIP and web conferencing
    • Cloud computing and cloud storage
    • The internet of things

B: The internet, and how devices exchange and store information

  • The internet

    Structure and infrastructure

    • A global network of interconnected computers
    • Points of Presence and Network Access Points
    • Clients, servers, routers, networks and connecting backbones
    • Connection methods: broadband, wireless and dial-up, and when each is appropriate
    • Internet protocols including TCP and FTP
    • The role and services of an Internet Service Provider
    • The relationship between bandwidth and transmission rates

  • The worldwide web

    Directly relevant to my web work

    • The web as the collection of information on internet-connected computers
    • The role of a web server
    • Website structure as hyperlinked pages
    • The components of a URL and what each does
    • The function of HTTP
    • HTML and the main features of its source code
    • How browsers request and display page components
    • Search engines and how they maintain indexes

  • Email

    Purpose, process and implications

    • What actually happens when an email is sent
    • Protocols: SMTP, POP3 and IMAP
    • The store and forward model
    • Advantages and drawbacks of email against webmail

  • Data exchange

    How data moves

    • Transmission modes: simplex, half-duplex and duplex
    • Codecs in VoIP, audio and video transmission
    • Transmission methods compared: fibre optic, wireless (infrared, microwave, satellite) and wire connectors
    • Parallel, serial and bi-directional transmission
    • Packet switching, and what a packet contains
    • Wireless network components: routers, access points and adaptors
    • Client-side and server-side processing, and the difference between them

  • Data storage

    Databases online

    • Database structure: tables, records, fields, data types and relationships
    • Online databases reachable over a network
    • The role of a database management system and structured query languages

C: Issues with operating online

  • Threats to data

    • Malicious and accidental damage, and when each occurs
    • Encryption, firewalls and anti-virus software
    • Passwords and levels of permitted access
    • Why data is backed up, how, and how it is recovered when lost

  • Operating safely

    • The benefits and the dangers of social networking and instant messaging
    • Managing your own online reputation
    • Email security: viruses, phishing and identity theft
    • How online technology can monitor people's movements and communications
    • How current legislation controls the use and protection of personal data

Where this shows up in my other work

The worldwide web topic is the theory behind the two sites I have built: URLs, HTTP, HTML source, how a browser requests and assembles a page. The data exchange topic is why the Unit 13 walkthrough video is 876 KB rather than 15 MB. The security topic is why this site has no form that quietly posts your details somewhere.
