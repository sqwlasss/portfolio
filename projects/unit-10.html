Unit 10 / Database Development

An appointment system for a GP practice

Handed out on 1 May 2026 and due on 24 May 2026. Nothing has been built yet, so this page is the brief and nothing more.

Briefed, not yet built

Read this first

There is no database, no design documentation and no submitted work for this unit anywhere in my portfolio. Everything below is what the assignment asks for, written in the future tense, because that is all that is true today. This page will be rewritten with the actual work once it exists.

The scenario

An established NHS practice in Crawley with five doctors and about 300 patients currently keeps patient and appointment information on a mix of paper and spreadsheets. A planned reorganisation of health services in the area will mean more patients and more doctors, so the managers have decided to upgrade.

They are deliberately not replacing everything at once. They want to start with the appointment system, because it lets staff get used to new software in an area of their work that has a limited risk to patient safety. My role in the scenario is an IT employee of the local health authority, asked to develop that system.

This is a coursework scenario, not a real NHS practice, and no work has been done for any real health organisation. Any data set I build for it will use obviously fictional names.

The four tasks

  1. Investigate how databases are used. Look at two databases designed for different purposes and present the strengths and weaknesses of both, as a PowerPoint with notes. The brief suggests health, employment, agencies, sale of goods, libraries and police as example domains.
  2. Design the database. Table designs, an entity relationship diagram, validation and verification procedures, forms, queries, reports, and a test plan with test data.
  3. Develop and test it. Build it, populate it, work through the test plan, record the faults and how they were resolved, demonstrate it to my tutor and record the feedback.
  4. Review it. Evaluate the finished database and make at least three recommendations for improvements that were not implemented.

What has to be in it

The hard numbers from the brief, split between what has to be designed and what has to be built.

Sorting — Single and multiple fields, alphabetic or numeric, ascending or descending

Automation — Automating tasks, for example using macros
Recommendations — At least 3 improvements identified but not implemented

One decision the brief leaves to me

The brief never names a database product. It asks for tables, forms, sub-forms, a main menu form, queries, reports, macros, primary and foreign keys, an entity relationship diagram, wildcards, and relational and logical operators, and it leaves the tool open. The only software it names anywhere is PowerPoint, for the Task 1 presentation.

What it does ask is that I reason about it: one of the Task 2 constraints is to consider the database constraints, e.g. software availability and whether this will have an impact on developing the relational database and whether there are any alternatives for developing the same solution.

So the choice, and the justification for it, is part of the work. I have not made it yet, and I am not going to name a product here as though I had.
