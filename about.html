About me

I am a BTEC Information and Creative Technology student, and I am looking for an IT apprenticeship.

What I like about this subject is the part where an idea becomes something you can click on. The two pieces of work I am proudest of are the ones where I took something from a brief on a page all the way to a finished thing: an eight-page website for a phone shop, and a ten-minute game review that started as a treatment and a storyboard.

The other thing I have learned is that writing the work up properly is not admin. My video had a risk assessment and a shooting log before it had any footage. My website had a storyboard and a test plan before it had any HTML. When I came back to build this portfolio months later, those documents were the reason I could say exactly what I did.

I built this site by hand in HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no framework and no build step, partly because Unit 3 asks for a portfolio and partly because I wanted to know I could.

How I work

Five habits that show up in every piece of coursework in this portfolio, because my briefs asked for them and they turned out to be worth keeping.

Plan on paper first. A treatment, a storyboard and a schedule before any recording. A storyboard and a site map before any markup. It is much cheaper to change your mind on a wireframe than in code.

Think about what could go wrong. My Unit 7 risk assessment scores six hazards and names a control for each. Two of them, backing up regularly and keeping a realistic schedule, are things I would do on any project now.

Test to a written plan. Deciding in advance what "working" means, then checking each one, catches things that clicking around does not.

Reference everything. My asset lists record where every image, sound and clip came from. It takes a few minutes and it means the work stands up.

Ask, then say what changed. The point of feedback is the edit you make afterwards. My Unit 7 evaluation names the comments I got and the specific changes I made because of them.
