About me

I am a first-year Digital Software Development T Level (Foundation Year) student at Crawley College, and I am looking for a 315-hour work placement.

What I like about this subject is the part where an idea becomes something you can click on. Before I started the course I was already doing that: I have a live art print storefront, a second one built and waiting to go live, an always-on server stack running on a Raspberry Pi in my house, and a Linux machine that serves a 35-billion-parameter language model locally. None of it is coursework, and all of it has taught me more than the assignments did.

The other thing I have learned is that writing the work up properly is not admin. My college video had a risk assessment and a shooting log before it had any footage. My college website had a storyboard and a test plan before it had any HTML. When I came back to build this portfolio months later, those documents were the reason I could say exactly what I did.

I built this site by hand in HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no framework and no build step, partly because the unit brief asked for a portfolio and partly because I wanted to know I could. It is the same discipline I bring to everything else: plan it, build it, test it against something written down beforehand, and write down what went wrong.

How I work

Five habits that show up in every piece of coursework in this portfolio, because my briefs asked for them and they turned out to be worth keeping.

Plan on paper first. A treatment, a storyboard and a schedule before any recording. A storyboard and a site map before any markup. It is much cheaper to change your mind on a wireframe than in code.

Think about what could go wrong. My Unit 7 risk assessment scores six hazards and names a control for each. Two of them, backing up regularly and keeping a realistic schedule, are things I would do on any project now.

Test to a written plan. Deciding in advance what "working" means, then checking each one, catches things that clicking around does not.

Reference everything. My asset lists record where every image, sound and clip came from. It takes a few minutes and it means the work stands up.

Ask, then say what changed. The point of feedback is the edit you make afterwards. My Unit 7 evaluation names the comments I got and the specific changes I made because of them.
