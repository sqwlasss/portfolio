Websites

Storefronts I have built. One is live; two are in development.

  • ddouble — art print storefront

    A live wall-art shop on React and Shopify

    A wall-art and homeware storefront with twenty routes: catalogue filters, product pages with click-to-zoom galleries, a persistent cart, a wishlist, and a full account area with order history and address management.   • ØBJECT 29 — rebrand, in development

    A full redesign of the same shop, not yet published

    A ground-up rebrand of the art-print store under a new identity. I rebuilt the visual system around a token layer, replaced every hardcoded colour with a token, and wired the design system into the linter so a regression fails the build. I split the largest page into four components without changing its rendered output, then restyled it. All fourteen routes were checked at phone width for horizontal overflow, contrast and focus rings were measured, and the five analytics events kept working through the rewrite. Total JavaScript fell from 806 KB to 795 KB. It is not live yet: the real purchase flow needs production Shopify keys, and the site still points at the old domain.

    • React 18 & Vite 6
    • design-token system
    • Tailwind & shadcn/ui
    • accessibility & reduced motion
    • GA4 events preserved
    • 14 routes verified
    • not yet deployed

  • Tessel — single-product storefront

    A Next.js storefront for a watch business

    A storefront on Next.js 15 and React 19, with a hand-written, strongly typed Shopify client instead of a framework. Checkout runs on Shopify's own page, so the business never handles card details, and incoming order webhooks are verified with an HMAC signature. A build step strips GPS and EXIF data from every product photograph, and a compliance check fails the build while any legally required business detail is still a placeholder. The brand name itself is provisional. Not published — it is developed against a mocked catalogue until the shop is connected.

    • Next.js 15 App Router
    • React 19, TypeScript strict
    • hand-rolled Shopify client
    • HMAC-verified webhooks
    • EXIF & GPS stripping
    • compliance build gate
    • not yet published

Systems & other work

The infrastructure and tools behind the sites, and the projects I keep for their own sake.

  • Always-on home infrastructure

    An always-on stack I run and maintain myself

    A Raspberry Pi running a Docker Compose stack with health checks, restart policies and pinned versions, fronted by a node manager I wrote in Flask that supervises services, probes their health, publishes Prometheus-style metrics and wakes or shuts down the other machines on my network. Four devices share the same working folders over a Syncthing mesh across two overlay networks, reachable by key-only SSH with no inbound ports open.

    • Docker & Compose
    • Python & Flask
    • Tailscale & ZeroTier
    • Syncthing, 4 nodes
    • PowerShell & Bash tooling

    Running

  • Local AI server — dual-booted desktop

    A 35B-parameter model running locally on 8 GB of VRAM

    I turned a Windows desktop into a Linux inference server by dual-booting it and resolving a firmware-level boot problem by hand. It now serves a 35B mixture-of-experts language model at roughly 47 tokens per second, and separately runs a local image generator. Both want the same eight gigabytes of VRAM and the machine does not have enough memory for both, so I wrote a service that switches the GPU between them on demand, plus a headless benchmark harness to measure the result.

    • Ubuntu 26.04, dual-boot
    • 35B MoE, Q4 quantised
    • ~47 tok/s, ~1.5s to first token
    • systemd mode-switch service
    • headless benchmark script

  • Face research pipeline

    A consent-gated image analysis system, written from scratch

    A self-hosted pipeline that runs on my own hardware behind a private network and never sends an image anywhere. It detects faces and produces embeddings, then scores candidate matches against a confidence model that only reports a match when more than one piece of evidence agrees. The published libraries would not build on my Python version, so I reimplemented the face detector and the embedding model directly in ONNX and checked them against the originals. Roughly 4,900 lines including the test suite.

    • Python, FastAPI, SQLAlchemy
    • ONNX face detection & embedding
    • 15-table schema, Alembic
    • async job queue, rate limiting
    • pytest suite
    • private-network only

How I work with AI coding assistants

I use AI coding assistants heavily and I think that is the honest way to work now. I use them to read unfamiliar code, to draft, and to get past the first version of something quickly. What I do not do is hand one over unchecked. Every project above has documentation recording what the assistant decided on my behalf, what is still unverified, and what I would change — and I test and debug the result before it goes anywhere near a user. If you would rather I worked without them on your placement, tell me on day one and I will.

How I work

Each of these is a habit I can point at a document for, not a quality I am claiming about myself.

  • I plan before I build

    Before recording a frame of my Unit 7 video I wrote a treatment setting out the purpose, audience and runtime, a ten-section script, an eight-panel storyboard and a four-week schedule. The edit followed the plan, and where it did not I wrote down why.

    See the planning documents

  • I assess risk before I start

    My Unit 7 risk assessment lists six hazards scored for severity and likelihood, each with named controls: taping down cables, ventilating equipment, surge protectors, backing up to cloud storage, and building a realistic schedule.

    See the risk assessment

  • I test against a plan

    For Unit 13 I wrote a five-test plan covering navigation, form submission, multimedia playback, responsiveness and external links, then tested the built site against it, repaired the faults and documented what changed.

    See the test plan

  • I reference where things came from

    My Unit 7 asset list has fourteen entries, each with a source reference, and my Unit 13 table of sources records the licence terms for every image, icon, sound and video used. It costs ten minutes and it means nobody has to take my word for it.

    See the table of sources

  • I act on feedback and say what changed

    Feedback on my video said it needed clearer speech, a slightly shorter length and better clarity in places. I cut unnecessary sections, improved the audio and refined the commentary, and recorded that in my evaluation rather than just agreeing with it.

    See the evaluation

  • I check my own work against the source

    Writing this portfolio up I found two rows of my own risk assessment where the printed rating does not equal severity multiplied by likelihood, and a scene in my shooting log dated 28/20/25. They are on the page, marked as errors, because hiding them would be the worse choice.

    See what I found
